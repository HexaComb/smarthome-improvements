Search "Anlin windows reviews" and most of what comes back is written by
dealers hundreds of miles away or by review sites that have never lifted a
window off a truck. Here is the version from a crew that installs them in
Fresno, Clovis, and Madera every week.

Our bias, stated plainly: we are a licensed local contractor (CA License

This article goes one level deeper, into the question homeowners actually ask
us at the kitchen table: Del Mar or Catalina?

## Anlin in one paragraph

Anlin Windows & Doors builds vinyl replacement windows and patio doors, and
only those. The company has been at it since the early 1990s, and its factory
and headquarters are in Clovis, California, which means the windows on our
jobs are built closer to your house than most pizza deliveries. Anlin
windows are sold and installed through dealers like us, not off a store
shelf. And the warranty is the headline:
a double lifetime warranty that covers the original owner for as long as they
own the home, transfers once to the next owner, and includes glass breakage.

## What is identical across Del Mar and Catalina

Before we split the two series apart, here is what does not change no matter
which one you pick:

- **The warranty.** The double lifetime transferable warranty applies across
  both series. Nobody is buying warranty coverage by moving up a line.
- **The factory.** Both are built in Clovis, with the same short lead times
  and the same local warranty service.
- **The glass options for our climate.** Both series can be ordered with
  glass packages that meet California's Title 24 energy code, and in Climate
  Zone 13 that code minimum is genuinely good glass. Blocking summer heat
  before it enters the room is the whole game here, and both lines can do it.
- **The install.** On our jobs, the same crew installs both, with the same
  permit, the same sealing, and the same cleanup. A racked frame or a skipped
  bead of sealant will ruin either series equally.

That last point matters more than any brochure. A big share of what people
call a "window review" is really an install review, which we will come back
to.

## Catalina: the value line

Catalina is Anlin's mainstream series, and calling it a "value line" needs
context: this is the value line of a premium vinyl specialist, not a
builder-grade product built to hit a new-construction price point. Anlin
does not make one of those, which is part of why we like the brand. There is
no bottom-shelf version to accidentally end up with.

Who Catalina fits:

- Homes where the budget has to stretch across many openings. On a 1980s
  tract home with 12 to 14 windows, the difference between series multiplies
  by every opening.
- Rentals and homes you may sell in the next few years. The transferable
  warranty travels with the house either way.
- Homeowners whose main goals are getting rid of single-pane aluminum,
  cutting heat gain, and getting windows that actually lock and slide. For
  that job, Catalina with a Title 24 compliant glass package is a big jump
  from what most Valley homes are living with now.

## Del Mar: the flagship

Del Mar is the line Anlin points to when a homeowner asks for their best.
Compared to Catalina it adds frame and glass upgrades. We are deliberately
not printing a spec table here, because series specs and package names get
revised, and a review with stale numbers is worse than no numbers. What we
will say is qualitative and holds up: the upgrades aim at the places where a
window works hardest.

Who Del Mar fits:

- West-facing walls that take the full force of a Fresno afternoon. If one
  room swings hot every summer evening, that wall is where an upgraded glass
  package earns its keep.
- Big glass. Large sliders and picture windows put more stress on frames and
  more square footage of glass between you and the sun.
- Noise. If you live on a busy street, ask what the glass upgrade options do
  for sound, and ask for the numbers rather than adjectives.
- The forever home. If you plan to be in the house for decades, the per-year
  cost of the upgrade gets small.

Could Catalina serve those homes too? Often, yes. This is a "which is worth
it for your walls" question, not a "which one is good" question. Both are
good. That is exactly why the decision should be made with current spec
sheets on your kitchen table, not with a blog post, ours included.

## What to ask at the in-home demo

Whoever quotes you, on any brand, make them put these on paper:

1. **The U-factor and SHGC of the exact window being quoted**, per series.
   These two numbers, on the NFRC label, are how you compare heat
   performance directly instead of trusting series names.
2. **The current glass package options** for each series and what each one
   adds, in numbers.
3. **Corner samples of both frames**, side by side, so you can see and feel
   the frame differences instead of taking a brochure's word.
4. **Both series priced on your actual openings.** A per-window price list
   for your real sizes turns "is Del Mar worth it" into simple arithmetic.
5. **The warranty in writing**, product, glass, and labor, and who answers
   the phone in year six.

If a salesperson cannot or will not produce those, that tells you something
useful too.

## Reading Anlin reviews the right way

Here is the thing about reviews of any dealer-installed brand: because the
factory does not install anything, every review mixes two products, the
window and the crew. The same window can sit behind a five-star review and a
two-star review, and the difference is usually measuring, sealing, and
cleanup, not the vinyl.

So when you read Anlin windows reviews, split the complaints. "The window
leaked at the corner" and "they left my stucco cracked" are installer
reviews. "The lead time" and "the warranty claim process" are closer to
factory reviews, and on those, a factory in Clovis is a structural advantage
for Valley homeowners: warranty service is a short drive, not a freight
claim.

The flip side applies to us. If you are vetting our crew, read our Google
reviews, ask for addresses of local jobs, and look at our
[completed projects](/projects/). Vet the dealer as carefully as the window.
