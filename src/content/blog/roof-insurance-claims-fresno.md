## The honest truth most roofers won't tell you

The single biggest reason a Central Valley roof needs replacing is not a storm.
It is age plus relentless sun. Our summers run hot for months, and that UV and
heat slowly cook a roof from the top down. Shingles dry out, lose their
granules, curl at the edges, and get brittle. That is normal wear and tear, and
it is exactly what a homeowners policy is not built to cover.

Insurance covers sudden, accidental events. It does not cover a roof getting
old. A 22 year old, sun baked roof that has quietly reached the end of its life
is a replacement you plan and budget for, not a claim you file. That is not a
loophole or an insurance company being difficult. It is how every standard
policy in California is written, and any contractor telling you otherwise is
either confused or steering you toward trouble.

So the real question is never just "is my roof damaged." It is "what caused the
damage, and can I point to the day it happened." That one distinction decides
almost every roof claim we see.

## Covered versus not covered

Here is the plain version of where the line usually falls. Your own policy is
the final word, so read it, but this is the pattern we see on Fresno and Clovis
roofs.

| Usually a covered peril | Usually not covered |
| --- | --- |
| A documented windstorm that tore or lifted shingles | Normal age and sun worn shingles |
| Hail that bruised or cracked the surface | Gradual granule loss and curling |
| A fallen tree or limb that punctured the roof | Deferred maintenance and neglect |
| Flying debris from a storm event | A poor or cheap prior installation |
| Sudden water intrusion caused by one of the above | Pre existing damage from before your policy |

The left column has a date. The right column happened slowly, over years. That
is the whole idea in one picture.

## When a roof insurance claim actually works

A claim has a real shot when you can tie specific damage to a specific event.
The strongest cases look like this:

- **A datable wind or hail event.** A storm rolls through, and afterward you
  have shingles in the yard, lifted or torn sections, or hail bruising that was
  not there the week before. The date of the storm is your anchor.
- **Impact damage.** A tree, a large limb, or wind driven debris strikes the
  roof and punctures or crushes it. The cause is obvious and sudden.
- **Sudden water intrusion from an event.** The storm opens the roof and water
  gets in fast, staining a ceiling or soaking insulation. That is different from
  a slow leak that has been dripping quietly for two years.

When you have one of these, documentation is everything. Write down the date of
the storm. Take photos as soon as it is safe, from the ground, of the roof, any
debris, and any interior water damage. The clearer the record that an event on a
known day caused the damage in front of you, the smoother the claim tends to go.
This is the honest side of insurance work, and it is the part we are glad to
help with. Our [storm damage](/roofing/storm-damage/) inspections exist for
exactly this situation.

## When a claim won't work (and can still cost you)

A claim usually fails when the real cause is time, not an event. Gradual wear,
old age, deferred maintenance, pre existing damage, and a poor prior
installation are all things a policy is written to exclude. No amount of framing
turns a worn out roof into a covered loss.

Once the old roofing comes off, the true condition shows itself. A lot of the
roofs we tear off were not storm victims. They were simply done, worn thin by
years of Valley sun, sometimes with old problems hidden under the surface from a
cheap prior job.

Here is the part that catches people off guard: filing a doomed claim is not
free. Even a claim that gets denied as wear and tear can still land on your
claims history, and a record of claims can follow you when your policy renews or
when you shop for coverage. So filing on a hunch, or because a salesperson
pushed you to, can cost you later without ever putting a new roof on your house.
That is why we do not encourage a claim unless there is a real event behind it.

## How a Fresno adjuster visit actually goes

If you do file, an adjuster from your insurance company comes out to inspect. It
helps to know how that visit works so nothing surprises you.

The adjuster gets on or near the roof, looks for the damage you reported, and
decides whether it matches a covered event or reads as wear and tear. If they
approve it, they write a scope, which is their list of what they will pay to
repair or replace and what they think it costs.

Two terms decide what actually hits your bank account:

- **Replacement cost value** pays what it costs to replace the damaged roofing
  now.
- **Actual cash value** pays that amount minus depreciation for the age and
  condition of your old roof. An older roof has more depreciation, so an actual
  cash value settlement on a worn roof can be a lot smaller than people expect.

Which one applies is written into your policy, not decided on the day. On top of
that, your deductible comes out of the payout. So an approved claim does not mean
the insurance company hands you a free roof. It means they cover their share of a
covered loss, after depreciation and after your deductible. Knowing that ahead of
time keeps the settlement from feeling like a letdown.

## What to do right now if you think you have storm damage

If a storm just came through and you are worried about the roof, here is a calm,
safe order to follow:

1. **Stay safe first.** Do not climb a wet, steep, or damaged roof, and stay
   clear of any downed power lines. No photo is worth a fall.
2. **Photograph everything from the ground.** The roof, any debris or fallen
   limbs, shingles in the yard, and any interior water stains. Note the date of
   the storm while it is fresh.
3. **Limit further damage if you safely can.** Small temporary steps to slow
   interior water, like catching a drip, are fine. Keep receipts for anything you
   spend on emergency measures.
4. **Do not let anyone start permanent work before the adjuster sees it.** If a
   crew tears into the roof first, you can lose the evidence your own claim
   depends on. Temporary protection is one thing. Permanent repairs before the
   adjuster is another.
5. **Call a licensed contractor for an honest inspection.** You want real
   documentation and a straight read on whether this is an event or normal wear.
