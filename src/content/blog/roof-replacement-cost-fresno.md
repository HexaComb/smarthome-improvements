## What actually moves the price

**1. Roof size and pitch.** Size is the biggest lever, and it is roof surface
that matters, not living space. A typical 4/12 to 6/12 pitch adds about 15
percent over the flat footprint; a steep 7/12 or greater pitch adds about 30
percent, plus harness-and-staging labor that slows the crew down. Nearly flat
roofs sit closest to the footprint.

**2. How many layers come off.** Tearing off one layer is included in the
brackets above. A second existing layer adds $75 to $150 per square for the
extra labor and disposal. If a previous owner overlaid instead of tearing off,
you inherit that cost. It is one of the quiet ways an overlay "savings" gets
paid back later, with interest.

**3. Decking condition.** Under the shingles is plywood or plank decking, and
we do not know its condition for certain until tear-off. Sound decking costs
you nothing extra. Rotted or delaminated sheets have to be replaced before
anything goes back on. We will not quote a fake flat number for this; it
depends entirely on what we find. What we do instead is put a clear per-sheet
replacement price in the written estimate before we start, so a surprise under
the shingles never turns into a surprise on the invoice.

**4. Flashing, penetrations, and details.** Chimneys, skylights, valleys, and
wall intersections take time to flash correctly. A simple rectangle roofs
cheaper than a cut-up roof with the same area, and reusing tired flashing is
one of the classic lowball moves (more on that below).

**5. Material.** Covered next, because it is the choice you actually get to
make.

## Tear-off vs overlay, and why we only do one of them

An overlay (nailing new shingles over the old ones) is cheaper up front
because it skips tear-off labor and dump fees. We do not offer it, for
reasons that have nothing to do with upselling:

- **You cannot inspect what you cannot see.** Overlays seal rotted decking,
  failed flashing, and old leaks under a fresh surface. The problems keep
  working in the dark.
- **Heat.** Two shingle layers hold more heat, and Central Valley summers
  already push roof surfaces to punishing temperatures. That heat cooks the
  new shingles from below and shortens their life.
- **Warranty.** Shingle manufacturers generally reduce or void coverage over
  an overlay.
- **The bill comes due anyway.** California building code generally limits a
  roof to two layers, so the next roof must tear off both, at the double-layer
  rate.

A tear-off costs more today and is the only version of the job we are willing
to put our name on.

## Shingle vs tile vs metal in the Valley

**Architectural shingle ($400 to $700 per square)** is the workhorse and what
most Fresno and Clovis homes carry. Modern cool-roof rated architectural
shingles handle Valley heat well, come in plenty of colors, and give you the
lowest installed cost. A quality shingle roof typically lasts 20 to 25 years
here.

**Concrete tile ($800 to $1,200 per square)** is the classic Central Valley
premium look and extremely durable, with a very long lifespan. Tile is heavy,
so the roof structure has to be verified for the load, and individual tiles
can crack under foot traffic, which makes future solar or HVAC work a little
more delicate. If your home already has tile, replacement often involves
reusing sound tile over new underlayment, which we price after inspection.

**Standing-seam metal ($800 to $1,400 per square)** costs the most at the top
end and earns it in lifespan and heat performance. Metal reflects sun rather
than soaking it in, sheds weather cleanly, and outlasts shingle roofs. If you
plan to stay in the house for decades, it is worth pricing both ways and
comparing cost per year instead of cost per job.

process details.

## Title 24 and the cool roof rule in Climate Zone 13

Fresno, Clovis, Madera, and Sanger all sit in California Climate Zone 13, one
of the hottest zones in the state's energy code. When you replace a roof
here, Title 24 generally requires the new roofing to meet cool roof
reflectance standards, verified through the permit.

What that means for you in practice:

- Your shingle needs to come from a rated cool-roof product line. Every major
  manufacturer makes them, and the shingle bracket in our pricing is already
  cool-roof rated, so this is built into the numbers above, not an add-on.
- It nudges color selection. The rated lines used to mean "light colors
  only," but current cool-roof shingles include grays, browns, and
  near-charcoals, so you are not stuck with a white roof.
- It is not just red tape. A roof that reflects more of the summer sun keeps
  the attic cooler, and in a town where the AC runs from May to October, that
  shows up in how hard your system works and what PG&E bills you for. We will
  not pretend to predict your exact savings; house, shade, and insulation all
  matter. Pairing a new roof with [proper attic insulation](/blog/how-proper-insulation-can-reduce-energy-costs/)
  is where the comfort really compounds.

A roof replacement requires a building permit in Fresno, Clovis, and the

believe them; they are telling you how they build.

## How to compare bids without getting burned

Three bids for "the same roof" can be hundreds or thousands apart because
they are not actually the same job. Put the bids side by side and check that
each one states, in writing:

- **The exact shingle product and line**, not just "30-year shingle."
  A three-tab shingle bid against an architectural shingle bid is not a
  comparison.
- **Full tear-off**, with the number of layers included, and what an extra
  layer costs if found.
- **A decking repair price per sheet**, agreed before the job, so rot found
  at tear-off has a known cost.
- **New flashing and pipe jacks**, not reused old ones. Reused flashing is
  invisible on day one and the source of the leak in year three.
- **Underlayment, starter strips, ridge caps, and ventilation** as part of
  the system.
- **Permit and Title 24 compliance.**
- **Disposal and cleanup**, including a magnetic nail sweep of your yard and
  driveway.
- **The warranty, in writing**, both the manufacturer's coverage and the
  contractor's own labor warranty, plus a CSLB license number you can look
  up. Ours is #1129883.

A lowball bid usually is not a better price. It is a shorter list.
